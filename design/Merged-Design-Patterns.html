01Empty StatesScreen-Empty-States.html
TutoringHQ · Empty states Lucide icons

The first thing every real center sees

Every center starts at zero. No students, no groups, no attendance taken, nothing collected. That state was drawn nowhere across the other 26 files, which meant it would have been invented separately on each screen it appears on. This is the pattern and every instance a center or teacher meets in their first hour. English and Arabic, each standing alone. Sample data is placeholder.

The pattern

Three parts, always in this order. What the screen is for, what happens once it is filled, and the one action that fills it. No illustration, no exclamation mark, no "Nothing here yet!". An empty screen is the one moment the product gets to explain itself, so it says the useful thing rather than the cheerful one. Where money is involved it also says that nothing is charged, because that is the question the owner is actually holding.

Rules, so these do not drift once they are built

  • One action, never two of equal weight. A primary button and at most one quiet alternative. Two primaries means the screen has not decided what the person should do.
  • Say what happens once it is filled, not what is absent. "Add them and attendance, fees and parent messages start working" beats "You have no students".
  • Where money could be a worry, say it is not. Nothing is billed until a session runs. That sentence removes the actual hesitation.
  • Empty because it is early gets the muted icon and no button. Insight and card orders are not waiting on anyone. Giving them a call to action invents work.
  • No illustrations, no mascots, no exclamation marks. They read as filler and they cost a screenful of space on a 748px phone.
  • Never a dead end. Every empty state either has an action or explains why none is needed.
  • Do not reuse the same copy across screens. Each one names its own screen, or it teaches nothing.

AR · إجراء بيتنفّذ

Rules

  • Show the shape of what is coming, not a spinner on a blank screen. A skeleton that matches the real row height means nothing jumps when the data lands.
  • Anything already known stays real. The header, the title, the back button. Only the unknown part gets a placeholder.
  • After a few seconds, say so. A quiet line beats a skeleton that has been sweeping for ten seconds implying something is about to happen.
  • Respect reduced motion. The sweep already switches off under prefers-reduced-motion and must stay that way.
  • Never a spinner in the middle of an empty screen. It tells the person nothing about what they are waiting for.
  • Do not turn a button into the word Loading. It keeps its label and dims, so the person still knows what they pressed.
  • Do not skeleton something that arrives instantly. A flash of grey is worse than no state at all.
  • A slow connection is not an error. Do not show a failure until it has actually failed.
